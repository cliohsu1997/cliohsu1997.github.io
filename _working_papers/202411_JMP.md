Abstract: We study the effect of return policies by asking whether they encourage efficient returns and purchases. A stricter return policy curbs inefficient ex-post returns, but the resulting lower return rates make higher product prices more attractive for sellers, leading to possible exclusion of ex-ante efficient purchases. We compare automatic coverage that reimburses return-shipping costs (RI) with optional coverage (CI) by estimating a structural model of purchase, insurance, and return decisions under monopoly pricing across six apparel categories. A lenient return policy like RI performs better in high-margin, high-salvage categories by expanding valuable purchases, whereas a strict return policy like CI performs better in thinner-margin categories by curbing excessive returns. Partial RPD, a menu pairing a discounted price with a medium restocking fee as penalty, performs better than RI and CI for most categories by better balancing purchase and return efficiency.
