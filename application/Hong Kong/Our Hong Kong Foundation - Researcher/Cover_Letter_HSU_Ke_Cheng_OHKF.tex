That is the kind of work I am trained for: build a model that ties evidence to theory and market structure, estimate it carefully on real data, quantify welfare or economic loss under counterfactual scenarios, and write up the result so a non-specialist can follow it---the same quantification skill set used in policy evaluation and in commercial disputes, valuation, and damages analysis.

The day-to-day described in the posting is close to what I already do. In my job market paper on Taobao apparel return policy, I clean large transaction data, estimate a structural model of purchase, return, and return-shipping insurance, rank policies by welfare, and decompose the loss into an ex-ante purchase margin and an ex-post keep-or-return margin. With Professors Kohei Kawaguchi and Kei Kawai, I clean and link large bookstore point-of-sale records and estimate pricing and inventory under demand uncertainty, where we compare a dynamic model against the classical static model and show the static version overstates marginal cost by about 20\%. Ph.D.-level industrial-organization coursework covered merger simulations, entry/exit counterfactuals, and firm-conduct identification. I run tested pipelines in Python, R, and C++ on SLURM and AWS, and I use Excel, Word, and PowerPoint for figures, summaries, and presentations. I work independently and in bilingual teams and meet deadlines.

I want to put this training to work on Hong Kong and Greater Bay Area policy questions, including economic development, land and housing, and related market and regulatory issues. My present compensation is a postgraduate studentship at HKUST; my expected monthly basic salary is HKD\,28,000--35,000. I attach my resume, transcript, and a writing sample (job market paper).
