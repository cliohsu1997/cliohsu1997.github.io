On learning and problem-solving. My research uses data to inform a decision a business faces. For an online apparel seller on Taobao, the decision was how lenient the return policy should be. A lenient policy, where the seller pays the return shipping fee, brings more sales but also more returns. A strict one, where the buyer pays, does the opposite. I cleaned and prepared the data in R and estimated which policy works better. Covering the fee suits a high-margin category such as tops. Letting each buyer pay suits a thin-margin one such as a basic T-shirt, where doing so improves welfare by about 50 percentage points. I then proposed a third option, a menu that lets the buyer choose between a lower price with a partial restocking fee and a full price with a full refund. This new policy beats both, reaching about 75 percent of the best attainable outcome in most categories. A back-of-envelope calculation suggests it could improve the seller's profit by about 5 million RMB. The same approach answers what a product team asks before a launch: how much the new product would sell, how much it would contribute to profit, and whether it would cannibalize sales of existing products. It can also show whether a policy raises profit without leaving some customers worse off, the key trade-off the Jockey Club faces. I work in Python, R, and C++, use AI for data management, coding, and generating weekly reports, and check the data before I trust a number.
