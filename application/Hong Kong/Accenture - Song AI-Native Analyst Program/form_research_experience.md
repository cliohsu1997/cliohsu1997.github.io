# Research experience (form paragraph)

As a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at HKUST, my research focuses on digital consumer behavior and retail markets. In my job market paper on Taobao apparel, I study how shoppers buy, insure, and return under different return policies: I clean large e-commerce transaction data, scrape and OCR product attributes in Python (including AI-based OCR for product images), estimate a structural model of purchase and return decisions, and compare policies by seller profit, consumer surplus, and welfare. With Professors Kohei Kawaguchi and Kei Kawai, I work on Japanese bookstore point-of-sale data—tens of billions of records across more than 1,000 stores—to analyze how pricing and inventory shape what customers can buy when demand is uncertain, and to show that ignoring inventory dynamics can overstate marginal cost by about 20%. Across both projects I build reproducible pipelines in Python, R, and C++ on AWS and HPC/SLURM, use AI carefully in data collection and coding, and present technical results clearly to non-specialists as a lecturer and teaching assistant.
