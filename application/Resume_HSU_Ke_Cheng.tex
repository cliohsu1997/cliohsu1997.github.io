{\large \textbf{Projects}}\\[-0.5pt]
\textbf{Inventory and Pricing under Resale Price Maintenance (RPM)} \textit{(w/ K.\ Kawaguchi, HKUST; K.\ Kawai, U.\ Tokyo)}\\
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Built a dynamic oligopoly model of pricing and inventory under demand uncertainty and RPM, with equilibrium stockouts and unsold returns (35--40\% of Japanese books printed are discarded); constructed title-by-market-by-month panels from monthly bookstore POS data (deliveries, sales, returns; tens of billions of records across 1{,}000+ stores), linked via standardized ISBNs and merged with local demographics and detailed title-level information scraped from Amazon in Python.
  \item Estimated demand and supply with instrumental variables (2SLS) and Hausman-style price instruments; validated the estimator with Monte Carlo on simulated equilibria; selected controls with LASSO and panel fixed effects; implemented iterative equilibrium simulation in C++.
  \item Revealed that the classical static model overestimates marginal cost by \(\sim\)20\% when ignoring dynamic demand uncertainty and inventory decisions; uncovered stockout-driven intertitle and intertemporal substitution (not interregional) through weekly descriptive statistics, regressions, and figures.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{-3pt}
\textbf{Return Policy Design in E-Commerce} \textit{(Job Market Paper)}\\
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Built and estimated a structural model of purchase, return-shipping-insurance take-up, and return decisions under monopoly pricing, using within-retailer switches between automatic provision and selective adoption of return-shipping insurance on Taobao (2017--2023; \(\sim\)62M RMB revenue; six apparel categories; \(\sim\)20--30\% return rates); scraped and OCR-digitized product attributes in Python.
  \item Developed a reproducible, version-controlled estimation pipeline on AWS and HPC/SLURM (Python, R, C++); cleaned and linked orders, prices, insurance, and returns; recovered category-specific demand, consumer fit uncertainty, and return responses via GMM; accelerated Monte Carlo and optimization in C++; validated policy rankings (seller profit, consumer surplus, social welfare) with bootstrap inference.
  \item Uncovered a purchase--return efficiency trade-off: a stricter return policy curbs inefficient ex-post returns but raises prices and can exclude ex-ante efficient purchases; demonstrated that automatic provision expands valuable purchases in high-margin categories while selective adoption outperforms it by \(\sim\)15--30 pp of first-best welfare in thinner-margin categories, and that a partial return-penalty discount (discounted price + \(\sim\)10--15\% restocking fee) attains \(\sim\)75--78\% of first-best welfare outside dresses.
\end{itemize}
