Hello, I'm Ke Zheng Shi. Please call me Cleo. It's my honor to be here. I'm a PhD candidate in economics at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, and I expect to graduate in June 2027. My fields are empirical industrial organization and the digital economy. The thing that runs through my research agenda is how people make decisions under uncertainty. By that I mean a choice made before uncertainty is resolved, when the later adjustment is costly. The early decision depends on the agents' anticipation of how much adjustment they need to make later. On the consumer side, the uncertainty is about product fit. If it is resolved before purchase, the problem is search. I'm interested in how consumers with different search costs search different products, and how the seller adjusts price, taking into account that some consumers switch from one type of product to another. If the uncertainty is resolved after purchase, the problem is returns.

The buyer receives the product, the match value is realized, and the buyer decides whether to keep it or send it back. Return policy changes how costly a return is and therefore the buyer's willingness to pay. I study which return policy generates a higher social surplus, and the answer differs across products. On the seller side, the uncertainty is about demand. Sellers choose price and inventory before they know how many consumers will buy. If demand exceeds inventory, there is a stockout. If demand falls short, there is leftover. Both are costly, so the seller makes the inventory decision in anticipation of that adjustment. One of my works is with Professor Kohei Kawaguchi at HKUST and Kei Kawai at the University of Tokyo. We study resale price maintenance, where the manufacturer fixes the price the retailer must charge. In the literature, RPM is pro-competitive because it encourages sellers to stock more. It prohibits them from lowering price on low-demand days, which prevents a price war, so sellers order more up front. But RPM also creates tremendous environmental losses, because when the price cannot be lowered, unsold inventories have to be discarded. Across projects, I ask the same question: how do people decide under uncertainty?
