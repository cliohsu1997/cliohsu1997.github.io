Preliminary result: Resale price maintenance is procompetitive because it encourages sellers to carry inventory in the low-demand state. When realized demand falls short of that inventory, however, the seller cannot lower the price to clear the market, and the unsold copies are returned at an environmental cost. To study this tradeoff, we build a dynamic oligopoly under demand uncertainty in which infinitely lived consumers substitute across periods and titles when their preferred title stocks out. In our model, sellers choose price and inventory before seeing actual demand, in contrast to the classical model, where price and inventory are set after demand is realized. We compare the marginal cost estimates between these two models. Our estimate identified from the inventory decision is roughly proportional to the stockout probability and is always positive, whereas the estimate recovered from the price condition can turn negative if demand is inelastic. Our estimate of production cost is lower than that classical estimate on average, because more weight falls on high-demand states, where demand is less elastic.
